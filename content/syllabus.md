## Course

DAD 492 — Topics: Programming for Sound Design. 3 credits. Spring 2027,
Dakota State University. No coding or DSP experience is expected.

Students design and build audio plugins in plugdata, a visual programming
environment based on Pure Data that runs inside a DAW. The course covers the
digital signal processing behind the standard effects and instruments (gain,
modulation, filters, delay, distortion, dynamics, reverb, and synthesis) and
ends with each student designing, exporting, and presenting original plugins.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course you will be able to:

1. Build audio effects and instruments in a visual programming environment.
2. Explain how common plugins (filters, delays, modulation effects, dynamics processors, reverbs, and synthesizers) process sound.
3. Design an original plugin from an idea to a working prototype.
4. Export a plugin in a standard format and test it in a DAW.
5. Document and present a plugin so other people can use it.

## Semester at a glance

The [schedule](/schedule/) has the day-by-day plan. In broad strokes:

- **Weeks 1–3** — Setup, Pd fundamentals, digital audio; utility and meter plugins
- **Weeks 4–5** — Oscillators and modulation; first export with the Heavy compiler; Project 1 assigned
- **Weeks 6–7** — Filters and EQ; Project 1 due
- **Weeks 8–9** — Delay and modulated delay; Project 2 assigned
- **Week 10** — Spring break
- **Weeks 11–13** — Distortion, dynamics, and reverb; Project 2 due
- **Weeks 14–16** — Instruments and the final project
- **Finals week** — Final project presentations

## Materials

No required textbook. All software is free:

- [plugdata](https://plugdata.org/download.html), with the Heavy compiler toolchain
- [REAPER](https://www.reaper.fm/) (free 60-day evaluation), or another DAW you already own
- Headphones

Readings come from free online books, chiefly Miller Puckette's *Theory and
Technique of Electronic Music*. The [resources](/resources/) page lists everything.

## Grading

Participation 20% · Plugin builds 30% · Projects 1 and 2 30% · Final project 20%.

90% and above = A · 80–89% = B · 70–79% = C · 60–69% = D · 59% and below = F.

- **Participation (20%)** — attendance, studio-day work, and feedback when
  classmates play their plugins.
- **Plugin builds (30%)** — the plugins built in class each week, collected in
  your [plugin portfolio](/assignments/plugin-portfolio/). Each one is graded
  complete/incomplete: it loads, it makes sound, and its controls do what they
  say.
- **Projects 1 and 2 (15% each)** — original plugins you design yourself. See
  [Project 1](/assignments/project-1-modulation/) and
  [Project 2](/assignments/project-2-hybrid/).
- **Final project (20%)** — an original plugin or instrument, exported and
  documented. See the [final project](/assignments/final-project/).

## Attendance and make-up policy

Students are expected to attend all scheduled classes. After three absences,
each additional absence lowers your participation grade.

Late work will only be accepted with a documented excuse. Students may redo work
they received an undesirable grade on once, but only if they turned in something
by the original due date. The deadline for redoing work is one week after the
original due date. After this time, no late work will be accepted.
